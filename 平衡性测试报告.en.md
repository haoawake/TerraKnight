# Balance Test Report

[中文](平衡性测试报告.md) | **English**

Test date 2026-10-14 (2.5.0); the data comes from the autotest `balance`. All values are calculated from the game's actual data and formulas: enemy HP, defense, damage per mask, the damage multipliers of elites and bosses, weapon damage and use time, armour defense, and the damage-taken formula. The section on the start of the game comes from real fights in King's Pass.

## How the numbers are calculated

- **Enemies**: the enemies of each stage are sorted by the Bestiary into three groups: regular enemies, elites and bosses. The tables give the median HP of the group, with the highest value in parentheses. Harmless and unkillable enemies are not counted. A boss's HP and defense are those of the Terraria boss it stands in for, tuned by its size and fighting style (see the "Bosses" section below and [阶段对照.md](阶段对照.md) (stage mapping)).
- **Weapons**: "best" is the weapon with the highest single-target output among those obtainable at this stage or earlier; "typical" is the median output of the weapons that appear at this stage. Weapons you hold down to channel have a different rhythm and are not counted, nor are the Nail and the Dream Nail, whose damage changes with the stage and the upgrades.
- **Single-target output**: damage per hit = weapon damage + damage of the best ammo available at that point − half the enemy's defense, with critical strike chance factored in. Melee counts one hit per swing; ranged and magic count one shot per use. Weapons with spread shots, piercing or multiple projectiles are therefore stronger in practice.
- **Player HP**: estimated from the extra masks, Life Crystals and Life Fruit you can roughly have at this stage (see the table below). Armour is the best head, body and legs obtainable at this stage, without set bonuses or accessories.
- **Damage actually taken** = (damage of one mask on Master difficulty − half your defense) × difficulty ratio, at least 1. Elites count at 1.5× and bosses at 2×.

## Bosses (Terraria boss values, tuned by size and fighting style)

Each boss first takes its HP (all parts and all phases added together) and defense from the Terraria boss it stands in for, using Terraria's Classic / Expert / Master formulas; Hard sits halfway between Normal and Expert. Its own life, defense and damage multipliers come on top, by its size and fighting style: big, slow and heavily armoured bosses have more life and defense, agile ones less life and defense and harder hits, and those that fight through summons or traps deal less damage themselves. "Damage" is the boss's damage relative to regular enemies in the same area (the global boss multiplier of 2× times its own). The "Stagger" column: a boss that can be staggered adds one to vanilla's stun counter for every 2.2% of its life it takes, much as the nail staggers it in vanilla; a boss with super armour is never interrupted.

Multi-phase bosses share their life out over the phases: the False Knight's armour takes 65% over its three rounds and his head 35% over his three times out; the Soul Master's and the Soul Tyrant's two phases take 60% and 40%.

Kill time assumes the strongest single-target weapon obtainable at the boss's stage, minus the boss's own defense, and is the theoretical fastest; typical weapons take several times longer. The last column is the HP in a Godhome rematch (Master, Attuned).

## Changes in 2.5.0

1. **Each boss has its own life, defense and damage by its size and fighting style** (see the "Bosses" section above). For example, Hornet has ×0.8 life, ×0.6 defense and ×1.2 damage, the False Knight ×1.15 life and ×1.5 defense, and Flukemarm ×0.7 damage.
2. **Staggers are paced by damage taken.** The bosses vanilla can stagger (Hornet, the Soul Master and Soul Tyrant, the Traitor Lord, the Hive Knight, the Broken Vessel and Lost Kin, Grimm and Nightmare King Grimm, the Hollow Knight, the Nailsage Sly, the Pure Vessel) add one to vanilla's stun counter for every 2.2% of their life they take; the other bosses have super armour the whole time.
3. **Multi-phase bosses share their life out over the phases**, so the whole fight has the stand-in Terraria boss's life, as a single-phase boss does.
4. The report's "best weapon" leaves out the Nail and the Dream Nail, whose damage changes with the stage and the Nailsmith's upgrades.

## Changes in 1.0

1. **Bosses use the HP of the Terraria bosses.** Every boss has the HP and defense of the Terraria boss it stands in for. Converting vanilla HP × 18.75, the way regular enemies are converted, would give the Radiance only 56,250 on Master (Moon Lord has 277,312) and Stage 4 Hornet only 8,437 (half of The Destroyer is 76,500). Late-game bosses are therefore much tougher than that conversion would make them, while early bosses stay close to it (False Knight 1,219 → 3,570, Hornet in Greenpath 4,219 → 4,641, from the converted value to the Terraria value).
2. **Drops are distributed by Terraria's progression.** The stage of all 1271 items is checked against each item's Terraria source and rarity, and 41 items take the stage this check gives them: Cattiva and Foxparks belong to Stages 2 and 3, not to the start; Life Fruit, Magic Dagger, Thorn Hook, Ice Bow and the Queen Slime drops in the Colosseum belong to Stage 4; Pulse Bow, Psycho Knife, Piranha Gun, Rainbow Gun and Ectoplasm belong to Stage 5. Each Stage 5 boss drops the items of its own part of the progression, and Moon Lord's gear is split by class among the four gods of Godhome.
3. **The Colosseum of Fools belongs to Stage 4, not Stage 3**: it lies inside Kingdom's Edge, and its drops are Hardmode items too.

## Worth noting, left as is for now

- **The start on Master difficulty is a grind.** The Copper Shortsword needs 38 hits (about 8 seconds) on the weakest enemy, the Crawlid, and the Wooden Bow needs 19 arrows. This follows directly from the rule "weakest enemy = two Master mode Blue Slimes". On Normal difficulty it takes 10 hits (about 2 seconds), about the same as fighting slimes in Terraria's Classic mode.
- **Late-game bosses last as long as Terraria's.** On Master, a Moon Lord-tier boss takes several minutes with a typical weapon, about the same as fighting Moon Lord in Terraria. If that feels too long, play one difficulty lower, or turn `Gameplay.BossDamage` down to make the fights more forgiving.
- **A rarity check for items that "appear too early" flags 48 items.** Most of the rest are high-rarity combined accessories and hooks that Terraria already has before Hardmode (for example Terraspark Boots, Lava Waders, Worm Hook); vanilla Terraria lets you get them before Hardmode, so they are not a problem.
