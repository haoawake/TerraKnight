## Features

- **Title screen**: in Chinese (Simplified and Traditional), the large main-menu title reads "空洞泰拉" (Traditional "空洞泰拉") in place of "空洞骑士", with the ornament, position and size matching vanilla; the subtitle of the Void Heart menu theme stays as well.
- **Terraria character**: the default Terraria character replaces the Knight, with standing, walking, jumping and swinging animations.
- **Start**: clicking New Game skips the prologue poem and the opening cutscene (after a black loading screen of about 5 seconds you go straight in), and the character appears right on the ground at the bottom of King's Pass: an Old One's Army Mysterious Portal swirls open, purple light dust spirals into it, the character fades in and walks out of the portal, and the portal then closes up and bursts into a ring of flashes (using Terraria's original portal sprites and sounds). A new save starts with a Copper Shortsword, a Copper Pickaxe, a Wooden Bow, the Nail (hotbar slot 4) and 10 Torches in the hotbar and 100 Wooden Arrows in the ammo slots, with a grappling hook in the hook slot of the "Misc Equipment" page, and Climbing Claws and a Cloud in a Bottle equipped.
- **Story moments**: the first time certain things happen, your character says a line or two and a tip tells you what to do about it, as a subtitle at the top of the screen and in the chat, once per save: your first fall and the shade it leaves (beat it to get the Geo back; on Master it keeps half), your first bench (life, respawn point, pylons), a pitch-dark room without a light (the lantern, glowing gear, Night Owl Potion), life below 30% (Quick Heal on H and Potion Sickness), a mask shard, Pale Ore (the Nailsmith), the Forgotten Crossroads infected, the Grimm Troupe in Dirtmouth, the Grimmchild (in the pet slot, the map marks the Nightmare flames), and all three Dreamers fallen (the Black Egg Temple opens), your first visit to the Forgotten Crossroads' shimmer spring (how the shimmer changes items). They are not part of the guidance and have their own switch in Terraria settings → Guidance; they wait for the Voice and the game's own talk to finish, and Creative saves start with them off.
- **Difficulty and Creative mode**: every new save starts with a choice of difficulty (Normal, Hard, Expert, Master, with the icons and colours from Terraria's world creation). Master uses the mod's base values; enemy health on Normal is a quarter of Master, on Hard half, on Expert three quarters (bosses use Terraria's own Normal / Expert / Master health, and Hard takes the midpoint between Normal and Expert). Enemy damage follows the same ratios: the damage after your defense is first worked out as on Master, then multiplied by the ratio, so however good your armour is, hits on Normal always deal a quarter of what they deal on Master. The save screen has a "Creative Mode" button at the bottom right: choose a save slot (an existing save asks you to confirm overwriting it once more), then a difficulty, and a Creative mode save starts in that slot. In Creative mode items never run out: getting something once gives you an unlimited amount (right-clicking a slot that shows ∞ takes one at a time without the slot going down, faster the longer you hold it, up to a full stack); the crafting menu makes any item for free, anywhere, at any time; shops cost nothing; your money stays at 99,999,999 (refilled as soon as you spend any); and the whole Bestiary is unlocked. A Creative mode save also: shows the whole world map (379 scenes; the atlas is generated from your own game files by the `layout` test and stored in `BepInEx/TerraKnight/worldatlas.bin`); unlocks the pylons beside all 40 benches, so you can teleport straight to places you have never been, and once you arrive the pylon moves next to that bench; puts a "Spawn" button at the top right of every enemy in the Bestiary: for a normal enemy or an elite, one click spawns it 10 tiles away in the direction the character faces (an enemy that is not in the current scene is borrowed temporarily from its own scene); **for a boss, one click sends you straight to its own arena to fight it from the start**: the opening cutscene, dialogue and story events trigger as in vanilla, even for bosses you have already beaten (before you enter, its "defeated" record is temporarily set back to unbeaten, you temporarily get the Dream Nail for the Warrior Dreams' spirits, and Grimm's troupe conditions are temporarily met; everything is restored when you leave the arena, and a win this time is recorded as a win). After a dream boss or a Godhome boss you return to the spot where you stood when you clicked the button. In a Creative mode save each of the four loadouts has its own inventory: switching loadouts (F1–F4 or the buttons beside the equipment slots) swaps the whole inventory along with the armour and accessories, and the four inventories are not shared; in a normal save the four loadouts share one inventory. A new Creative mode save comes with showcase gear for recording videos: the four loadouts wear the Solar Flare, Vortex, Nebula and Stardust armour respectively, with the best accessories for each class; the first inventory slot of each class holds that class's best weapon (Zenith, S.D.M.G., Last Prism, Terraprisma), the second slot holds the class's lunar pickaxe, and after them come the class's other showcase weapons and the Lunar Hook; the ammo of the ranged inventory sits in the ammo slots; Life Crystals, Life Fruit, Mana Crystals and all permanent boosts are used up to the maximum. When a Creative save started with an earlier version is loaded, its existing inventory goes to the current loadout, and the other three loadouts each get their own class's showcase inventory. Creative mode also treats the three paid benches (City of Tears, Queen's Gardens, Ancient Basin) as already paid for, and its Hallownest bag holds every Hallownest item: the Lumafly Lantern, the City Crest, the four keys (4 Simple Keys), the Tram Pass, the King's Brand, the Delicate Flower, 21 Rancid Eggs, 6 Pale Ore, the four relics (14 Wanderer's Journals, 17 Hallownest Seals, 8 King's Idols, 4 Arcane Eggs), the Void Heart, the Grimmchild and the awoken Dream Nail; they are topped up on every load, so whatever was used up or sold comes back. In a normal save you pick items up one at a time and can craft only with materials already in your inventory. Save slots show "Creative Mode" and the difficulty.
- **Language**: follows the language chosen in Hollow Knight (English, French, German, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Spanish, Korean, Japanese, Italian, Portuguese, Russian; Terraria has all 11 of them), and the mod switches along when you change the language in the game menu. Terraria item names, tooltips, buffs, NPC names and dialogue are read straight from the official language files inside your local Terraria.exe (not packaged with the mod). Tooltips the mod changes are handled line by line: lines that match Terraria's originals use the official translation, and the mod's own content and interface text use the mod's bundled translation table (`Resources/lang.json`, written in Simplified Chinese and translated into the other 10 languages); area names use Hollow Knight's own official names.
- **Terraria gameplay**:
  - Hollow Knight's nail, dash, spells, Focus, double jump and wall jump are all removed. Movement abilities come from Terraria accessories; the Dream Nail is an item in your inventory (see "Dream Nail" below).
  - 100 health and 20 mana, with Terraria's red hearts and mana stars at the top right. Damage you take is converted from masks into Terraria damage (each mask hurts more in deeper areas); health regenerates naturally and refills fully when you sit on a bench.
  - Movement feels like Terraria: run speed, acceleration, jump height and fall speed are all converted from Terraria's own values, and there is fall damage (the drop built into vanilla when you enter a room through a side or bottom door does not count; boss rooms, where you cannot dig or build, do not count either).
- **Digging and placing**: scene terrain is made of 0.5-unit tiles, and every tile can be dug and placed. Dug-out spots reveal Terraria background walls whose colour follows the area: King's Pass and the Forgotten Crossroads have blue-grey cave walls, Greenpath jungle walls, Queen's Gardens flower walls, Fog Canyon Pearlstone walls, Fungal Wastes orange sandstone walls, City of Tears blue Dungeon Brick, Royal Waterways green Dungeon Brick, Crystal Peak Amethyst walls, Resting Grounds purple-grey cave walls, Deepnest Spider walls, Kingdom's Edge Marble walls, The Hive Hive walls, Ancient Basin Granite walls, The Abyss and the Black Egg Temple Obsidian walls, White Palace Pearlstone Brick, Godhome Gold Brick, dreams Hallowed Sandstone walls, the Grimm Troupe tent Crimstone walls, Colosseum of Fools Lihzahrd Brick, and Dirtmouth, Howling Cliffs and everywhere else stone walls. Digging throws off Terraria debris, and turf and stone ledges that stick out from terrain edges are removed too once the ground beneath them is dug out; vines, grass, bushes, moss and leaves growing on, hanging from or lying in front of a dug tile go with it whole, so no half vine is left hanging in the air (after a reload too).
- **Smart Cursor** (toggle with Ctrl): following Terraria's source code, the pickaxe picks the block to mine by itself and highlights it in yellow. Mining to the left or right digs a walkable three-tile-high tunnel column by column (in each column the chest-height tile first, then the one at your feet, then the one above your head), and mining diagonally carves out stairs. With the Smart Cursor on, the chests within reach (the world's, the areas' supply chests, your own) get a grey outline and the one nearest the cursor a gold one; a right click (when the cursor isn't on another thing to use) opens it, as Terraria's Smart Interact does.
- **Auto Select** (hold Shift; Terraria's Auto Select): when the cursor points at a block within reach of your pickaxe, your hand switches to the pickaxe, and when it points at air (or at a block out of reach), it switches to a torch; when the cursor is more than 12.5 tiles away from you, the last stack of torches in your inventory is used, and if you are already holding torches nothing changes. The switch happens only after the current use finishes, and releasing Shift switches back to the original item.

Items whose sprite is a strip of animation frames (Fallen Star, souls, food) draw only the current frame in the inventory, on the ground and in hand, playing at vanilla's frame count and speed (Fallen Star flickers back and forth over 8 frames; food stays on the first frame).

    - Hits on enemies carry only Terraria's effects, without Hollow Knight's nail sparks, screen shake or "clink" parry sound, and they do not bounce you back the way the nail does. Knockback works the Terraria way: a hit gives the enemy a velocity based on the weapon's knockback, the knockback resistance of the enemy's area and whether it was a critical strike, launching it backward and upward (flying enemies drift backward), and it lands under Terraria's gravity and then skids to a stop. A launch's length grows roughly with the square of its push, and Hallownest's rooms are smaller than Terraria's surface, so the push (weapon knockback × the enemy's knockback taken, ×1.4 on a crit) grows at 45% of the rate above 3.5 px per tick (an early broadsword's) and stops at 6.5: high-knockback weapons such as the Enchanted Boomerang and the Spear still throw farther than a broadsword, but not across half the room. An enemy knocked into the air takes no further push until it lands (a flying enemy, until it has drifted back), so projectiles that land several hits a second, such as the Zenith's swords and the Star Wrath's stars, do not carry it across the room; those hits still count toward its run of knockbacks. Five knockbacks within 3 seconds steady a common enemy for 1 second: it takes no knockback for that second and is drawn half see-through, back to normal when the second ends. An enemy on the ground is pushed as in Terraria's NPC.StrikeNPC: a heavy hit (damage ×10, or ×15 in Expert and Master, exceeds its maximum life) only tops its velocity up to that hit's knockback; a light hit sets a smaller velocity directly (multiplied by the knockback resistance once more). Only bosses and elites are not knocked back. Elites are decided by enemy type, from the base health that kind of enemy has in Hollow Knight: sturdy enemies with 70 or more, such as Husk Guard, Hive Guardian, Stalking Devout, Great Husk Sentry, Kingsmould and the Colosseum's Heavy Fool, 21 types in all, including the Hunter's Journal mini-bosses Vengefly King, Soul Warrior and Pale Lurker. Enemies that in Hollow Knight stay in place when hit and are unaffected by gravity take no knockback either.

- **Currency**: all buying and selling uses Geo. **Geo is a physical item in your inventory** (its icon is the coin of the Geo counter at the top right); one stack holds up to 2,147,483,647, and it can be dropped on the ground, put into chests or stored in the **Piggy Bank** (sold by the Merchant for about 100 Geo; place it on the ground and right-click to open 40 slots of private storage, and every Piggy Bank holds the same contents). Vanilla Geo drops and is picked up as usual, and the Geo you pick up becomes Geo in your inventory; enemies drop one and a half times the game's Geo, times the difficulty's bonus (Hard ×1.25, Expert ×1.5, Master and Hollow ×2). The Geo counter at the top left shows the total of your inventory plus the Piggy Bank: Stag Stations, toll benches, Sly's shop and the Dirtmouth shops all go by that total, taking from the inventory first and then from the Piggy Bank for whatever is missing. Selling earns a fifth of the buy price. Items with no value in Terraria (Dirt Blocks, Stone Blocks, wood and the like), and items made only from such things (Work Bench, wood armor…), sell for nothing. Geo can't be sold. Hover text appears to the right of the cursor, level with its tip, so the cursor never covers it.

- **Full-screen map** (M): a Terraria-style world map, drawn tile by tile from the actual colliders: ground, structures (colliders placed by hand in the scene), platforms, every kind of block you place, torches, ropes and dug-out spaces, with water, spikes, acid and lava drawn in open space, coloured by area. Benches, lamp posts, doors (labelled with their room when it is known, such as Sly's shop or Nailmaster Mato's hut), levers, lifts, chests, Stag Stations, toll machines, trapped Grubs, Geo deposits, Soul Totems, items on the ground, breakable walls, hot springs, NPCs and your own chests are drawn as coloured squares; hover the mouse over one to see what it is (ground shows its area, blocks show the item name). Exploration records only cells within eight tiles of the player that are not occluded by terrain; entering a room never reveals the whole camera view or room. Unexplored cells stay hidden without a purchased map. Buying an area map from Cornifer or Iselda shows unexplored cells at 70% transparency (30% opacity); exploring them makes them fully opaque. The full-screen map, minimap and overlay share the same exploration record. On first loading a legacy TerraKnight_slotN.map, the new version creates a .before-exploration-v2.bak backup and resets old exploration bits, preserving purchased maps, terrain and buildings. New exploration persists across reloads; the Life Crystals in them show only once you have seen them yourself. All scenes are joined at their doors into one map, matching how you walk through them in the seamless world (Hollow Knight's rooms do not fit together perfectly on a global scale, so the two sides of some doors are slightly offset on the map; rooms behind doors (shops, houses, the Grimm Troupe tent) are drawn in the empty space beside their door, but only once you are inside that room, so you never see places that do not exist hanging in the sky above Dirtmouth; places with no door connecting them to anywhere else, such as the White Palace, dreams and the Tram, are drawn above the whole map). The map opens centred on your player head; the scroll wheel zooms around the mouse, dragging pans (zooming out or dragging onto rooms not drawn yet, they come in a few each frame, the middle of the screen first, instead of stalling; while you play, the map is also drawn ahead a little at a time), clicking a pylon icon teleports you, and your shade's position is marked with Terraria's death marker. The map built from Hollow Knight's map pieces is also available: turn off `Gameplay.TerrariaMap` in the config to use it. As in Terraria, Tab cycles through three display modes (the choice is remembered): a minimap in the top-right corner (with Terraria's gold frame, whose buttons zoom in, zoom out and reset), the whole map laid semi-transparently over the screen, and no map. Numpad +/- (or = and -) zooms the current mode and PageUp/PageDown adjusts its opacity; hold them to change continuously. Opening the inventory tucks the minimap away so it does not cover the equipment slots. Tab switches the display mode of the Terraria map. The map has two layers: the seamlessly joined world is the shallow layer, and the rooms reached only by a door, a dream or a tram (shops and homes, the Colosseum, the Grimm Troupe's tents, the Archives, the White Palace, the dreams, the trams) are the deep layer, drawn beside their doors; the minimap and the overlay draw the player's own layer, and the full map's "Current / Shallow / Deep" tabs at the bottom left choose the layer shown (the current one when it opens). The player's head is drawn on the middle of the body.
- **Pylons**: every pylon can be knocked out with a pickaxe and drops as its pylon item; set down on open floor elsewhere (3 tiles wide and 4 tall, within reach) it is a new pylon, on the map and travelled to as usual. Not inside a boss's fight, a dream or Godhome; a bench's pylon knocked out doesn't grow back by sitting on that bench again. Travelling to a moved pylon goes into its room by the bench nearest it (made the respawn point) or, without one, by the room's entrance, then out of the portal beside the pylon. Every bench has a Terraria teleportation pylon beside it, but the pylon appears only after you have sat on that bench once, and pylons you have not unlocked are not shown on the map ahead of time. Where the space beside a bench is too narrow (it cannot fit a pylon 3 tiles wide and 4 tiles tall), the search widens in distance and height; if there is still no room, the pylon is placed right next to the bench, so every bench gets one. The pylon's style changes with the area (Forest, Jungle, Cavern, Ocean, Mushroom, Hallow and so on), and it glows. Walk up to a pylon and right-click to open the full-screen map, then click any unlocked pylon icon on the map to teleport straight there; it takes about 1 second from the click until you can move. You arrive standing beside the pylon, stepping out of a portal that opens and closes quickly, and the portal's colour depends on the area (green in Greenpath, blue in the City of Tears, pink in Crystal Peak, red for the Grimm Troupe…). You walk out standing (the same holds when you teleport straight from sitting on a bench). After death you respawn at that bench; if the bench cannot be used (for example a toll bench you have not paid for), your respawn point stays at your previous bench. The game looks up the respawn bench by name and accepts only active objects; when it finds none, TerraKnight uses an inactive bench with the same name, the pylon's position or a door in the scene (vanilla would put you outside the scene in an endless fall). When a teleport is very fast, vanilla's fade-in event can get lost; TerraKnight sends it again, so the screen fades in as usual. Unlike in Terraria, teleporting does not require that there are no enemies nearby or that an NPC is near the pylon.
- **Enemy stats** (the figures below are for Master difficulty; for the other difficulties see "Difficulty and Creative mode" above): the baseline is Master mode's Blue Slime. Hollow Knight's weakest enemies (Crawlid, Vengefly, Tiktik, 8 HP in vanilla) have 150 Terraria HP, equal to two Master mode Blue Slimes (25 × 3 × 2). Other enemies scale in proportion to their own vanilla HP (18.75 per point), and enemies deeper in are already tougher in vanilla: one with 15 vanilla HP has 282 (×2 more in Kingdom's Edge and the Abyss, and ×5 more for regular enemies and elites in the endgame areas). **A boss takes its life and defense straight from the Terraria boss it stands in for**, using Terraria's own difficulty scaling (Normal / Expert / Master, with all body parts added together): in Master, False Knight has King Slime's 3,570, Hornet in Greenpath has the Eye of Cthulhu's 4,641, Broken Vessel has Plantera's 53,550, the Hollow Knight has Golem's 76,500, and Godhome's four gods and the Radiance have the Moon Lord's 277,312 (Oro and Mato share theirs). Bosses fought together split one Terraria boss between them (the three Mantis Lords share Skeletron), and in Godhome, rematches against bosses from earlier stages are scaled to endgame strength. On top of this, enemy life is scaled by stage: common enemies and elites 50% in Stages 0 to 3, 31.25% in Stage 4 and 20% in Stage 5; bosses 65% in Stages 0 and 1, 60% in Stage 2, 55% in Stage 3, 50% in Stage 4 and 40% in Stage 5 and Godhome rematches. See [阶段对照.md](../阶段对照.md) (stage reference table) for every number. The deeper the area, the higher enemies' defense (2 / 4 / 8 / 10 / 20 / 30; each hit is reduced by half the defense), the more they resist knockback, and the more damage each mask of their attacks converts to (20 / 30 / 42 / 58 / 76 / 100). The six tiers are roughly: King's Pass, Dirtmouth, Forgotten Crossroads → Greenpath, Howling Cliffs → Fungal Wastes, Fog Canyon, Queen's Gardens, Crystal Peak, Royal Waterways → upper City of Tears, Deepnest, Ancient Basin, The Hive, Resting Grounds → Kingdom's Edge, The Abyss → White Palace, Godhome, dreams.
  - Bosses and elites: bosses deal 2× the damage of regular enemies in the same area and elites deal 1.5× (their projectiles included; adjustable in the config), while regular enemies deal the base damage. Bosses and elites take no knockback. Elites have super armour the whole time. The bosses vanilla can stagger (Hornet, the Soul Master and Soul Tyrant, the Traitor Lord, the Hive Knight, the Broken Vessel and Lost Kin, Grimm and Nightmare King Grimm, the Hollow Knight, the Nailsage Sly, the Pure Vessel) add one to vanilla's stun counter for every 2.2% of their life they take, much as the nail staggers them in vanilla; the other bosses have super armour the whole time (vanilla adds to a boss's stun counter with every hit, and Terraria weapons hit several times a second, which would interrupt them again and again). Each boss is also tuned by its size and fighting style: big, slow and heavily armoured bosses have more life and defense, agile ones less life and defense and harder hits, and those that fight through summons or traps deal less damage themselves (the numbers are in the balance report). Multi-phase bosses share their life out over the phases: the False Knight's armour takes 65% over its three rounds and his head 35%, and the Soul Master's (and Soul Tyrant's) two phases take 60% and 40%. Elites are mini-bosses (Vengefly King, Pale Lurker, Soul Warrior), unique enemies (Royal Guard, Elder Baldur and others) and regular enemies with more than 70 vanilla HP (Husk Guard, Hive Guardian, Shrumal Ogre, Great Husk Sentry and others).
  - Health bars follow Terraria: an enemy shows a small health bar below it only after it has lost health (a config option can make it always visible), and a nearby boss shows a large health bar with exact numbers at the bottom of the screen.
  - Hovering the mouse over an enemy shows "Name: current HP/max HP" beside the cursor, as in Terraria; the name is the Chinese name from Hollow Knight's Hunter's Journal (dream bosses use their own title names, such as Lost Kin).
  - The Zenith works as in Terraria: each swing sends out three swords (the Zenith itself plus two random component swords, which go after enemies near the cursor) that swing out from your side to the target along an ellipse and loop back, each with its own coloured trail, afterimages and tip flash.
  - The grappling hook works as in Terraria: press E to throw it toward the cursor; once it catches a block it pulls you over, and Jump releases it (if you are barely moving, you jump off it). Mining the block it holds unhooks it. While hooked or being pulled, your Cloud in a Bottle double jump, Rocket Boots fuel and Flying Carpet are refilled (as in Terraria). It releases automatically when you die, land in spikes or change scenes, so it is never left hanging mid-pull, and it never changes your character's gravity.
    - All 27 hooks (R7): Ivy Whip, Dual Hook, Web Slinger, the six gem hooks and the Amber Hook, Skeletron Hand, Bat Hook, Spooky Hook, Candy Cane Hook, Christmas Hook, Fish Hook, Slime Hook, Anti-Gravity Hook, Tendon Hook, Thorn Hook, Illuminant Hook, Worm Hook, Lunar Hook, Static Hook, Squirrel Hook and Hook of Dissonance, each with its Terraria range, shoot speed, retract speed, pull speed, number of hooks that can latch at once, chain sprite and glow. E uses the first hook in your inventory. The gem hooks, the Ivy Whip and the Lunar Hook are crafted with their Terraria recipes; the rest drop from the corresponding bosses / enemies.
- **Combat feel**:
  - Hitting an enemy shows Terraria's effects: damage numbers, blood particles, and Terraria's hit and death sounds, without Hollow Knight's white slash flash and burst.
  - Getting hit works as in Terraria too: you are knocked back (4.5, 3.5 pixels/frame) but keep control, get 40 frames of invincibility with Terraria's blinking rhythm, blood particles and Terraria's hurt sound.
  - On death your character breaks into three pieces (head, body, legs) that fly off and fade out, blood fills the screen, and "You were slain..." rises in the middle.
  - There is no Hollow Knight hitstop (the brief freeze of time) when you get hit, kill an enemy or pull a lever; Terraria has none.
- **Behind the foreground**: when your character walks behind foreground art, a dimmed silhouette with a faint gold outline shows where you are.
- **Geo deposits**: weapons break them, and a pickaxe can also strike one with a click (each hit drops one portion of Geo, as with the nail). Each deposit gives 150 Geo in all, shared between its hits and its breaking (a five-hit one 25 a hit and 25 as it breaks).
- **Invisible walls at the world's edge**: like the invisible walls at the edges of a Terraria map, when you walk (fly, dig) to a side of a scene that does not connect to another scene, such as the sky above Dirtmouth or the outer side of the cliffs, you are stopped at the edge of the scene and cannot leave the world. If that side has a door (leading to a scene that cannot be joined seamlessly, or one of the game's own transitions), you can walk through it as usual.
- **Seamless world (experimental, off by default since 2.3.1)**: by default scenes change as in vanilla Hollow Knight (a loading transition at each door). Set `Gameplay.SeamlessWorldExperimental` to `true` in the config to turn it on: scenes connected by left/right and top/bottom doors are joined into one continuous map. Neighbouring scenes load in the background ahead of time, with enemies and mechanisms active as usual, right on the other side of the door, where you can see them and dig into them. Crossing a door line (or falling or digging across one) has no loading, no black screen and no pause; your character keeps moving at the same speed, and the camera stays centred on them. Tiles you dig in the neighbouring scene stay dug. In vertical shafts joining two scenes (such as the well in Dirtmouth), the black foreground of the upper scene that covers the shaft is cut away, so you can see all the way down the lit shaft of the lower scene, and the brick walls the lower scene draws on both sides of the shaft are shown right up to the seam. Near a door line the colour grading of the two scenes blends gradually, so colours never jump as you cross. Hollow Knight has a kind of invisible wall that blocks enemies but not the player (Enemy Blocker), which vanilla uses to keep enemies out of hidden passages and away from room edges. Crawling enemies that touch one do not turn around (crawlers only follow terrain) and keep pushing against it, and flying enemies chasing you stop in mid-air, as if they had hit an invisible wall. These walls are turned off when a scene loads (neighbouring scenes loaded seamlessly included). The ones guarding doorways stay only while no scene is joined on the other side of the door (to keep enemies from walking out of the world); once the door is joined to the neighbouring scene they are turned off too, so enemies on both sides can walk and chase across and never get stuck at the seam, visible but unable to get through. If an enemy has followed you into the neighbouring scene and that scene is unloaded, the enemy stays where it is and moves on when that scene loads again. In boss fights, dreams and the Colosseum of Fools, all of these walls stay in place. When two doors on the same side are not in line (on the left of the Howling Cliffs entrance, the door to Howling Cliffs is at the very edge and the door to the Stag Nest is three tiles further in), the scene behind the inner door never blocks the outer door, and walking toward the outer door never hands you over to the scene behind the inner door. Only doors whose directions match are joined: an exit leading to a door elsewhere (such as the door on the side of Sheo's hut that leads to the middle of Greenpath) does not lay that scene over the hut. Standing on the seam between an upper and a lower scene does not flip you back and forth between them, and the camera does not drift; at the moment you cross a door line, the camera is not dragged away by the new scene's camera lock zones. Rooms with an extra boss scene, doors, bench teleports and respawning after death use vanilla transitions. When a neighbouring scene loads in the background, its state machines (enemies, mechanisms) are initialised gradually over the following frames, and the loading frame itself usually takes 20–40 ms or less (King's Pass is the heaviest, at about 70 ms).
- **Terrain feel**: Terraria's automatic step-up (walking into a one-tile ledge steps you up onto it); Hollow Knight's slopes become steps and are just as walkable. Terrain colliders placed by hand in scenes (roofs, wall blocks, floating platforms) are merged into the tile grid too, so you can dig through them; once a floating piece of terrain is dug out completely, its art disappears with it. Spikes and thorns grow on terrain: the parts you dig away vanish along with their art and cannot hurt you (thorns hanging in mid-air with no terrain still prick). Explosions (bombs, dynamite and the like) blow away the spikes and thorns in their reach too, and those the blasted ground held up; once a cell is dug or blown out, the open cells beside it left holding only spike art go with it, so no stub of a spike is left, and places dug in an older version are tidied when you enter the room; the map draws spikes only on the cells that really prick. Spikes and thorns hurt by Hollow Knight's share: each prick takes one mask's worth of your life (a fifth of it with five masks), whatever your defense, halved by the Dead Man's Sweater; Greenpath's and the Queen's Gardens' thorns also poison you for 10 seconds as Terraria's poison darts do (two life a second, no regeneration; the Bezoar and the Medicated Bandage keep it off). When you slide down a wall holding toward it, you go straight into any hole dug in the wall as you pass it. Releasing Jump only stops the upward boost and keeps the upward speed you already have; jumping partway through a grappling hook pull launches you with its momentum, and you can follow up with a Cloud in a Bottle double jump.
- **Other details**: when you fall from the sky at the start, the camera stays inside the scene; in a scene you walk into seamlessly from next door, state machines that vanilla keeps off stay off (such as the disused door at the Stag Station that leads to the Black Egg Temple); if you die before resting at any bench, you return to where you first appeared (the ground where the portal stands in King's Pass, just as Terraria sends you back to the world spawn when you have no bed; benches you have not sat on do not count as respawn points, and the vanilla respawn point is on the ledge at the Dirtmouth entrance, which has neither a bench nor a pylon); mechanisms meant to be hit with the nail, such as bells, levers, jars, vines and breakable walls, can be triggered by every Terraria attack (swords, shortswords, projectiles, spells, whips, yoyos, minions, sentries; at most once every 0.25 seconds per mechanism), and hitting things that make vanilla's "clink", such as spikes, armour, hard shells and breakable walls, does not bounce you back the way the nail does (the Nail item does); the nail's side and up slashes don't throw you back off enemies or their shields either, only off such things, and the down slash still bounces off enemies; you land firmly on a platform from a height (the player uses continuous collision detection), the grappling hook can latch onto platforms, and bosses and elites pass through platforms the player places (they never get stuck on them); lifts, elevators, patrolling enemies and other things that work by coordinates line up correctly in joined scenes too; when you dig into one of vanilla's hidden rooms from another direction, the mask covering the room is lifted automatically.
- **Saving**: the tiles you have dug and placed in each scene, your inventory (including favourite marks and what is in the trash can) and your unlocked pylons are saved together with the Hollow Knight save (when you sit on a bench or quit to the main menu).
- **Multiplayer (LAN, direct IP)**: modelled on Terraria's "Host & Play" and "Join via IP"; enter it through "Multiplayer" in the bottom-left corner of the save screen. See [Multiplayer](#multiplayer) below for details.
- **Terraria settings**: above "Settings" in the bottom-right corner of the inventory there is "Terraria Settings" (and above it "Wiki", which opens the TerraKnight wiki, haoawake.xyz/terraknight-wiki, in your browser), with "Autofire" from Terraria's settings menu (hold the mouse button and every weapon keeps swinging and firing; Terraria's `Main.SettingsEnabled_AutoReuseAllItems`), how enemy health bars are shown, "Camera lock" (on, the character stays in the middle of the screen as in Terraria; off, Hollow Knight's own camera), and key bindings like Terraria's controls menu (Dash defaults to F and Shadow Dash to X; click an action and then press the new key, Esc cancels, "Reset to Default" restores them). "Settings" opens Hollow Knight's own settings menu.
- **Mouse cursor**: the "Cursor" tab on the right of "Terraria Settings" changes the mouse cursor: its style (Terraria's cursor, with the smart cursor's look while that is on, or Hollow Knight's own ornate cursor), size (50%–300%), color (hue, saturation and lightness bars as in Terraria; "Default color" returns to the style's own), glow (0% no rim, 25% Terraria's own light rim, higher adds a halo of the cursor's color) and pulse on or off, with a live preview on a dark and a light ground; "Restore defaults" goes back to Terraria's cursor as it is. The choices are kept in the [Cursor] section of the config file.
- **Interface scale**: the Interface page on the right of Terraria settings has Terraria's UI Scale bar, 50% to 200%: the mod's whole interface (inventory, life and mana, map, chat, tooltips, the settings panel) larger or smaller, applied when you let go; when the screen can't hold it, the largest it holds is used (the actual scale shown). "Reset to 100%" goes back. Kept in the config as `Interface.UIScale`.
- **50 save slots**: below the title of the save screen there are page controls ("‹ Page 1 / 13 ›"; PageUp / PageDown or the scroll wheel also work). Each page has Hollow Knight's usual 4 save buttons, and the number on each button is its slot number (the last page has slots 49 and 50). Save files are named as in vanilla (`user5.dat` ... `user50.dat`), and the save screen opens on the page you last played. The Creative mode panel lists the 4 slots on the current page. (Slots 5 and above need version 2.1.0 or later.)
- **Fall damage when wall sliding**: while you slide down a wall (Climbing Claws, Shoe Spikes), the starting point of the fall is recalculated every frame (Terraria's `WallslideMovement`), and when you land at the bottom of a slide, fall damage is calculated from the slide.
