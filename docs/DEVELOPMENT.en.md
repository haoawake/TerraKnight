## How it works

- Only Hollow Knight runs; a BepInEx plugin implements Terraria's systems inside it in native code.
- Terraria's textures and sounds are **read at runtime from the Terraria installed on your own computer**. The mod itself ships no Terraria assets, so you need to own and install both games.
- Terrain: the scene's original tk2d collision outlines are read, rasterised into tiles and replaced with chunked composite colliders. Dug-out areas cut Hollow Knight's ground art away with a SpriteMask, and the mod draws the black fill itself, tile by tile.
- Seamless world: on entering a scene, the scenes behind each of its doors are loaded additively in the background (real scenes, with their scripts and enemies running), and before any of their scripts start, each whole scene is moved to the other side of its door (the two doors overlap; left and right doors also line up the ground below the door). This side of the door line belongs to this scene, the far side to the neighbour. The neighbour's scene-entry settings (music, colour grading, darkness level, area title, shade) are held back and only run when the player actually walks in; its PlayMaker state machines are also paused and then initialised one by one, a few milliseconds per frame (actions such as item pickups, which pre-build a prompt UI for every item type, build it only when it is used). Crossing a door line does not go through Hollow Knight's scene-transition flow; it just makes the neighbour the current scene (the active scene, the scene manager, the map and the scene name in the save all switch with it), unloads the scenes that stop being adjacent, and loads the new neighbours in the background. Vanilla transitions such as doors, benches and death first unload all neighbours and then enter the new scene through the vanilla flow. In a moved scene, whenever a state machine reads or writes coordinates (gets a position, sets a position, moves to a point, spawns an object at a point), the coordinates are converted to that scene's own coordinates, so logic with hard-coded numbers, such as lifts and patrol ranges, works as usual.

## Requirements

- Hollow Knight Steam version 1.5.12620 (the current release, Unity 6)
- Terraria (1.4), found automatically in the same Steam library by default; if it is not found, set `TerrariaDir` in `BepInEx/config/com.haoawake.terraknight.cfg`
- BepInEx 5.4.23.x (win x64)

## Configuration

`BepInEx/config/com.haoawake.terraknight.cfg`:

| Key | Description |
|---|---|
| `Paths.TerrariaDir` | Terraria folder; leave empty to find it automatically |
| `Gameplay.TerrariaMovement` | Terraria movement feel; turn it off to keep Hollow Knight's feel |
| `Gameplay.CenteredCameraExperimental` | Experimental, off by default: a Terraria-style camera that keeps the character in the exact centre of the screen every frame, ignoring the room's vanilla camera lock zones and the scene edges (best used together with the seamless world). When off, the camera is Hollow Knight's vanilla camera |
| `Gameplay.SeamlessWorldExperimental` | Experimental, off by default: connected scenes (left/right, up/down) join into one continuous map and load ahead of time. When off, scenes load at each door as in vanilla |
| `Gameplay.TerrariaMap` | Terraria-style world map (drawn tile by tile from the colliders, revealed wherever you go); turn it off for the map assembled from Hollow Knight's map pieces |
| `Gameplay.MapStyle` | How the map is shown: 0 hidden, 1 minimap at the top right, 2 overlaid on the screen (saved automatically when you switch with Tab) |
| `Gameplay.HollowKnightDarkness` | Keep Hollow Knight's darkness: without the Lumafly Lantern, dark areas (the edges of Howling Cliffs, Deepnest, the mine tunnels of Crystal Peak, etc.) shrink your view to a small circle around the character that slowly darkens. Off by default: the view does not shrink (with Terraria lighting on, these places are still dark the Terraria way) |
| `Gameplay.TerrariaLighting` | Terraria-style lighting (on by default): light is computed per tile; torches, the Mining Helmet, bullets and spells, and pylons light up the surrounding scene softly, tile by tile, and light fades quickly through blocks; dug-out tunnels, placed blocks and the unlit parts of dark areas are dark, while open space and terrain in vanilla rooms look as they do in vanilla. Turn it off for plain glow halos |
| `Gameplay.EnemyHealthBarsAlways` | Always show enemy health bars; off by default, so, as in Terraria, a bar appears only after the enemy takes damage (can also be toggled in "Terraria Settings") |
| `Gameplay.AutoReuseAllItems` | The "Autofire" option in Terraria Settings: every weapon keeps swinging and shooting while you hold the mouse button; off by default (can also be toggled in "Terraria Settings") |
| `Multiplayer.PlayerName` | Your name in multiplayer; leave empty to use your Steam nickname |
| `Multiplayer.Look` | Your look (colour scheme) in multiplayer: 0 is the colours of Terraria's default character, 1-7 are other schemes |
| `Multiplayer.LastAddress` / `Multiplayer.Port` / `Multiplayer.MaxPlayers` | The address you last joined, the port (default 7777), and the maximum number of players when hosting (default 8, up to 16) |
| `Multiplayer.SyncEnemies` | In multiplayer, players in the same scene share one set of enemies (on by default); turn it off and everyone fights their own |
| `Keys.*` | Terraria key bindings (Unity Input System key names, such as `W`, `Space`, `LeftShift`, `Digit1`, `F1`); easier to change in game in "Terraria Settings" |
| `Gameplay.EnemyMultiplier` | Multiplier for the number of normal enemies, default 2 (double); 1 is the vanilla count |
| `Gameplay.BossDamage` | Boss damage as a multiple of the damage of normal enemies in the same area, default 2 |
| `Gameplay.EliteDamage` | Elite damage as a multiple of the damage of normal enemies in the same area, default 1.5 |
| `Gameplay.SkipOpeningCutscenes` | A new game skips the opening prologue poem and the intro cutscene; once it has loaded you walk straight out of the portal in King's Pass. On by default |
| `Gameplay.EnemyRespawnScale` | Multiplier for how long normal enemies wait before respawning: the wait is tiered by strength (30 / 45 / 70 / 100 seconds, 4 minutes for elites) and multiplied by this value; default 1, 0 for no respawns (the older setting `EnemyRespawnSeconds` set to 0 also means no respawns) |
| `Video.TargetFrameRate` | Frame rate locked during play, default 144 (turns off V-Sync); 0 uses the game's own setting |
| `Dev.InfiniteItemsAllSaves` | For development: every save works like Creative mode, with items that never run out, a fully unlocked crafting menu and no Geo needed (also on during autotests). The `Dev.InfiniteItems` setting from earlier versions has no effect |
| `Dev.AutoTestSlot` | For development: 1-50 automatically enters this save slot after launch to run tests and take screenshots; 0 turns it off |

## Development

```
# First time: copy the path config and change it to your Hollow Knight folder
copy LocalPaths.props.example LocalPaths.props

# Build; the dll is copied automatically to <Hollow Knight>/BepInEx/plugins/TerraKnight/
dotnet build src/TerraKnight
```

- `tools/XnbDump`: decodes Terraria's .xnb textures into PNG, batch-verifies texture and sound decoding, and renders tile-framing test images, without launching the game.
- Autotest (`Dev.AutoTestSlot`): starts a new game or loads a save in the given slot, skips the intro cutscene, and tests in King's Pass:
  - accessory feel (boot speed-up, shield dash and bash, Cloud in a Bottle double jump, with speeds logged in Terraria pixels/frame), wall sliding with Climbing Claws;
  - Smart Cursor digging order, the Zenith's flying swords, the grappling hook latching on and pulling you;
  - the pickaxe striking a Geo deposit, digging a pit, placing blocks, standing and falling, swinging weapons at enemies and props, mining away hand-placed terrain colliders;
  - walking through the right door into Dirtmouth (how long the switch frame takes, whether the vanilla transition ran, whether the tiles dug there are still there), walking back and then on further in, dropping down the well into the Forgotten Crossroads (enemies in the neighbour scene are moving), crossing the line leftwards from the Crossroads;
  - taking a hit (knockback, invulnerability, flashing, no hit-stop), then dying once (after respawning, standing still does not flip back and forth between scenes, and the shade stays in the coordinates of the scene where you died);
  - walking back from Dirtmouth to King's Pass, being blocked by the unbroken great door, and cutting it open.

  Several further separate suites (`Dev.AutoTestSuite`): `v10` tests the 1.0 corrections (the invisible wall and the left door above Dirtmouth, whether an Elder Baldur curled up in its shell takes hits from arrows and minions, the minimap at the White Palace and Greenpath entrances, rooms behind doors drawn only while you are inside them, the bosses' Terraria stats), `layoutmissing` investigates only the scenes that do not yet have a position on the map (leaving the world atlas untouched), `bossgates` lists, for each boss arena, the save records that decide whether that fight takes place (what the `arena` challenge button relies on), `ammoslots` tests the ammo slots (the order in which ammo is searched, which slot a pickup goes into, that only ammo fits, that bows use the ammo slots first), `arena` tests the boss challenges in the Creative mode Bestiary (entering, whether the boss is there, the records restored as they were after leaving, returning to where you were after dream and Godhome fights), `fixes` digs out a floating platform to see whether its texture disappears with it, touches dug-out thorns to see whether they hurt, and enters Sheo's hut to see whether Greenpath gets overlaid into it; `features` uses the crafting menu and inventory with every kind of click, swings every sword at enemies (sword beams, flashes, damage), points the mouse at enemies to see their name and health, lays down a Flying Carpet, and sits on the bench in Dirtmouth to unlock the pylon and then teleports back; `visual` crosses several left/right and up/down door lines, logging changes in picture brightness and camera offset frame by frame, then stands on a seam to see whether it flips back and forth; `tuning` pylons (in the game's order: sit down first, and the respawn point is set only after a while), enemy strength and the super armour of bosses and elites (compared with vanilla hit reactions), respawns and corpses, the Radar; `loadout` switching between the four loadouts, shared accessories and save migration; `summon` summon staffs, minions, whips and whip tags; `progression` item Stages and sources, recipes, Geo remainders, enemy doubling and respawns, kills counting toward the Bestiary and drops, Dirtmouth NPC dialogue, buying, selling and buyback in shops, with screenshots of the town, the dialogue boxes, the shops and the Bestiary; `platform`, `ammo`, `weapons` and `accessories` log the stats of platform stairs, all ammo, the new weapons and the new accessories side by side with vanilla Terraria (for example how far a spear thrusts, how high each extra jump rises, how high wings climb, how fast wall sliding is). `bossrun` fights the world's 20 bosses and elites the way normal play does: it enters through each boss room's own door, walks close or into the trigger zone to wake the boss, presses Up before the challenge prompt (Mantis Lords), then attacks only with the mod's own attacks (invulnerability frames still count, and it keeps hitting even when blows land on armour) until the game itself records the kill; it logs the time taken, the drops, whether a health bar is left on the corpse, whether you can act afterwards, and errors along the way (`bossrun_A+B` fights only some of them); `water` falls into Flukemarm's pool and jumps back out, once before the fight and once after it dies.
  
  Screenshots are saved in `BepInEx/TerraKnight/dev/` and the log is `BepInEx/LogOutput.log`; Unity's own errors are in `Player.log` in the game's save folder (BepInEx does not write them to LogOutput.log by default).
- Trailer (`promo/`):
  - The footage is recorded by the autotests. First version (30 fps): `promo` the four classes each fighting a Master mode boss, the Dirtmouth well, the map and pylons, placing crafting stations; `promo2`, `promo3` Soul Warrior, digging down, Path of Pain, the Gravity Globe. Second version (50 fps, exactly one physics step per frame, for smoother motion): `promoA` starts at the save screen (clicking "Creative Mode", choosing a save slot, pointing at each of the four difficulties in turn, choosing Master) and the portal start in King's Pass, then the NPCs in Dirtmouth, talking to the Merchant and buying something, the Bestiary (opening it, pointing at a few entries, selecting one, clicking "Spawn", then switching the same entry from Normal to Master to show how its stats and drop rates change), the crafting menu, and switching between the four loadouts; `promoB` nine bosses (hovering on wings, keeping distance, smooth aim transitions, each class switching to weapons not yet used), drops in the Crossroads, sixteen weapons used in turn; `promoC` the whole Path of Pain room (a breadth-first search over the hazards' real shapes plans the route, then it flies the whole way at constant speed along a smooth curve), the Lunar Hook, digging down, the Gravity Globe, teleporting by clicking a pylon on the map; `promoD` records only weapons; `promoE` records only map pylons; `promoF` opens the map in the City of Tears and clicks a pylon (the icons are on screen, and the arrival after the teleport is recorded too). UI actions use a test-only pointer (`TerraCursor.DevDrives`): the cursor glides along a curve onto a button, pauses, then clicks, and hover effects work just as with a real mouse. Frames are in `BepInEx/TerraKnight/dev/rec/<clip>/`, the encoded clips in `promo/clips/` (`encode_clips.py`), and contact sheets in `promo/sheets/` (`timesheet.py` makes ones with timestamps).
  - `make_music.py` synthesises the soundtrack `music.wav` (original 140 BPM electronic music, with no samples of any kind) and the beat map `beats.json`; `edl.py` is the edit list (for each shot: which clip it comes from and at what second it starts, how fast it plays, how it is cropped, how it transitions, and the captions, all timed to the beat; the same stretch of time from the same clip is never used twice, which the script checks; the first version is `edl_v1.py`); `make_promo.py` renders the edit list frame by frame into `TerraKnight_promo.mp4` (`--range 30 40` renders just one stretch to preview it, `--endcard` renders only the end card); `make_jianying.py` uses the jianying-editor skill to turn the same edit list into a JianYing draft "泰拉圣巢宣传片" (TerraKnight trailer) that you can keep editing in JianYing. Chinese version: record the `zh_` clips with `promoA_zh`, `promoB_zh`, `promoC_zh` and `promoF_zh`, then `PROMO_LANG=zh python make_promo.py` produces `TerraKnight_promo_zh.mp4` (the same edit; `ZH_SHOT` in `edl.py` changes only the shots whose timing shifted after re-recording), and `PROMO_LANG=zh python make_jianying.py` produces the JianYing draft "泰拉圣巢宣传片（中文版）" (TerraKnight trailer, Chinese version).
  - The English text on the end card uses the Cinzel font (`promo/assets/`, SIL Open Font License).
