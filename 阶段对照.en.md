# Stage Map: Terraria's Progression ↔ Hollow Knight's Route

[中文](阶段对照.md) | **English**

Terraria and Hollow Knight both play out roughly along a single line. This document explains the six stages TerraKnight divides Hollow Knight's full route into, which stretch of Terraria's progression each stage corresponds to (which bosses are down, what gear you should have), and which Terraria boss each Hollow Knight boss plays in this mapping: its **life and defense are that Terraria boss's**, and **it drops that boss's (or that stretch of progression's) items**.

A stage decides three things:

- **How strong enemies are**: defense, damage per mask and knockback resistance rise with the stage; a boss's life is taken directly from the Terraria boss it stands in for.
- **What drops**: each enemy type's drop table is set by the stage it belongs to.
- **What you can buy and craft**: shop stock and crafting station recipes unlock by stage.

In the code, this rests on `LevelOf` in `src/TerraKnight/World/Areas.cs` (which stage a scene belongs to); the bosses' Terraria stats are in `Items/BossStats.cs`, the drop tables in `Items/EnemyDropData.cs`, and the shops in `Items/TownShopData.cs`.

## How you enter a stage

**By the route along which the Knight gains abilities** (since 2.3.0). In Terraria, beating a boss opens the next stretch of progression; in Hollow Knight, getting a new ability opens the next part of the map. So the stages follow the abilities:

| Obtained | Unlocks | Terraria equivalent | Where to go at this point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mothwing Cloak (defeat Hornet in Greenpath) | Stage 1 | Eye of Cthulhu defeated, Shield of Cthulhu obtained | Fungal Wastes and Mantis Village, Fog Canyon, Howling Cliffs |
| Mantis Claw (Mantis Village) | Stage 2 | Eater of Worlds / Brain of Cthulhu defeated | City of Tears, Royal Waterways, Crystal Peak, Resting Grounds |
| Crystal Heart (Crystal Peak) | Stage 3 | Skeletron defeated, getting ready to go down to the Underworld | Deepnest, Beast's Den, Ancient Basin, Queen's Gardens |
| Monarch Wings (defeat Broken Vessel in Ancient Basin) | Stage 4 (Hardmode) | Wall of Flesh defeated | Kingdom's Edge, The Hive, Colosseum of Fools, The Abyss |
| Shade Cloak (The Abyss) | Stage 5 | Plantera defeated | White Palace, Godhome, the dream realm, Grimm Troupe, final battle |

Your unlocked stage equals how many of these five you have obtained. When a stage unlocks, its NPCs move into Dirtmouth, the shops get new stock and the crafting station recipes open up; NPCs who have moved in never leave. The Guide's dialogue hints at what to look for next.

Enemy strength and drops follow the area the enemy is in (not your unlocked stage), and the areas are divided along the same route. Hollow Knight is not strictly linear; you can reach Deepnest early, for example. If you go early, the enemies there still have their higher-stage strength and drop higher-stage items. It is the same as going down to the Underworld or into the Dungeon early in Terraria.

The three dream bosses (Failed Champion, Soul Tyrant, Lost Kin) can be fought once you have the Dream Nail. From 2.3.1 they are placed by the point at which they become fightable: they stand in for Skeletron (Stage 2), the Wall of Flesh (Stage 3) and The Destroyer (Stage 4) respectively, and so do their Godhome challenges of the same name.

Each monster's drop table is set per monster (`Items/EnemyDropData.cs`) and does not follow the 2.3.0 area split: monsters in Fungal Wastes and Fog Canyon drop Stage 2 items, monsters in the upper City of Tears and the Resting Grounds drop Stage 3 items, and monsters in The Hive drop Stage 3 items. So drops in these places are one stage ahead of or behind the stage the area belongs to.

Stage placements set in 1.0:

- **The Colosseum of Fools is in Stage 4**: it lies inside Kingdom's Edge, and the Oblobbles drop Queen Slime and Hallowed Mimic items (Daedalus Stormbow, Volatile Gelatin, Hook of Dissonance…), all of them Hardmode.
- **Cattiva and Foxparks** (the summons from Terraria 1.4.5's Palworld crossover) do not drop from the start of the game: Cattiva drops from Mossy Vagabonds in Queen's Gardens in Stage 2, and Foxparks from Mawlurks in Ancient Basin in Stage 3. Their "Trusty" versions need the Hardmode Kinship Peach and can only be crafted in Stage 4. In vanilla Terraria they are Pink and Light Red rarity items, far too strong to have at the start.
- **Life Fruit** drops only in Kingdom's Edge (in vanilla Terraria it grows only after a mechanical boss is defeated). The Magic Dagger is a Mimic-family drop in Kingdom's Edge, the Crimson weapons (The Meatball, Flesh Grinder, Deathbringer Pickaxe) follow the Brain of Cthulhu's stage, and Ectoplasm drops only in the White Palace (the late Dungeon).
- Post-Plantera items stay out of Stage 4: the Pulse Bow, Psycho Knife, Piranha Gun and Rainbow Gun are all in Stage 5.

## Boss mapping

Terraria's bosses matter because beating them hands you the key to the next stage: the Eye of Cthulhu gives Demonite Ore, the Eater of Worlds gives Shadow Scales, the Wall of Flesh starts Hardmode, the three mechanical bosses give the three Souls, Plantera opens up Golem and the various events, and last comes the Moon Lord.

In each table below, the life figures are that Terraria boss's life on **Normal / Expert / Master** (all parts added together), which is the Hollow Knight boss's life in TerraKnight. The calculation is the same as Terraria's `NPC.ScaleStats`: base life × difficulty multiplier (Normal 1, Expert 2, Master 3) × that boss's own Expert adjustment × 0.85 for Master. TerraKnight's "Hard" difficulty sits halfway between Normal and Expert.

- Bosses fought together split one Terraria boss: the three Mantis Lords share Skeletron, the six Watcher Knights share the Wall of Flesh, the two Oblobbles share Queen Slime, and Oro and Mato share the Moon Lord.
- Worm bosses (Eater of Worlds, The Destroyer) count for only half: their life is spread over dozens of body segments, and in Terraria a piercing weapon hits several segments at once, while here there is only one body.
- Lesser bosses in the same stage get a share of that boss (given in brackets, e.g. "80%").
- Defense is also that Terraria boss's (each hit is reduced by half the defense).
- **Rematches in Godhome**: in Godhome's rooms, bosses from earlier stages are converted to the level of the Lunatic Cultist (61,200 on Master) according to the share they hold in their own stage, because by the time you go to Godhome you already carry endgame weapons. The extra HP that the Ascended tier adds over Attuned is added on top as in vanilla (up to double). Dream bosses, and Godhome bosses that already belong to Stage 5, are not converted.

**The gear of the Martian Saucer, Betsy and the Empress of Light** is in Godhome too: the Martian Saucer's (Influx Waver, Charged Blaster Cannon, Xeno Staff, Laser Drill…) goes to Nailmasters Oro & Mato, Betsy's (Flying Dragon, Sky Dragon's Fury, Aerial Bane, Betsy's Wrath, Betsy's Wings) to Paintmaster Sheo, and the Empress of Light's (Kaleidoscope, Terraprisma, Starlight, Nightglow, Eventide, Stellar Tune, Empress Wings, Soaring Insignia) to the Pure Vessel.

**The Moon Lord's gear is split by class among the four gods of Godhome**: melee goes to Sly, ranged to Oro and Mato, magic to Sheo, summon to the Pure Vessel. Like Terraria's Celestial Pillars, each god also gives the lunar fragment of its own class, which you can take to the Ancient Manipulator to make that class's lunar armor, weapons, wings and tools. The Radiance drops only the Moon Lord's class-neutral items. White Palace enemies (Wingmoulds, Kingsmoulds) are like the small enemies around the Pillars: they occasionally drop any of the fragments, as well as late-Dungeon weapons (Shadowbeam Staff, Spectre Staff, Inferno Fork, Magnet Sphere, Kraken, Pulse Bow, Piranha Gun, Rainbow Gun…).

## Enemy stats

| Stage | Defense (each hit reduced by half of it) | Damage per mask | Knockback taken |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 2 | 20 | 75% |
| 1 | 4 | 30 | 65% |
| 2 | 8 | 42 | 60% |
| 3 | 10 | 58 | 55% |
| 4 | 20 | 76 | 45% |
| 5 | 30 | 100 | 40% |

- **Life of normal enemies and elites**: each point of vanilla HP equals 18.75 Terraria life (Master), so enemies deeper in the game are tougher to begin with. Crawlids and Vengeflies (8 HP in vanilla) have 150, equal to two Master-mode Blue Slimes; husks (15 HP) have 282. Stage 4 enemies get a further ×2 and Stage 5 enemies a further ×5 (vanilla enemies there have about as much HP as earlier ones, while the player already wields Hardmode weapons). Other difficulties scale in proportion: Normal is a quarter of Master, Hard is half, Expert is three quarters.
- **Boss life and defense**: taken from the Terraria boss it stands in for; see the tables above.
- **Bosses and elites**:
  - Bosses deal ×2 damage (config `Gameplay.BossDamage`) and elites ×1.5 (config `Gameplay.EliteDamage`); their projectiles use the same multipliers.
  - Bosses and elites have super armour throughout: they are never stunned or interrupted and take no knockback.
  - Elites are mini-bosses (Vengefly King, Pale Lurker, Soul Warrior), unique enemies (such as Kingsmould and Elder Baldur), and normal enemies with 70 HP or more in vanilla (Husk Guard, Hive Guardian, Shrumal Ogre, Great Husk Sentry and so on).
- **Normal enemy damage** follows the table above, with no extra bonus.
